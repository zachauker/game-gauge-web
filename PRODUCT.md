# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Any gamer — from completionists who track everything they play to casual players deciding what to try next. Primary context: after a session, on desktop or couch, wanting to log what they played, check what friends are playing, or find their next game. No single persona; the app serves the spectrum.

## Product Purpose

Game Gauge is a gaming journal — a personal record of what you've played, how you rated it, and what you thought. Built around IGDB data and Steam integration, it lets users rate, review, and organize games into lists while seeing what their community is playing. Success looks like: a user who can glance at their profile and see their entire gaming history, trust the ratings because they came from thoughtful people, and feel like the app gets out of their way.

## Brand Personality

Serious, thoughtful, personal. Like a private journal that happens to be connected — intimate, honest, a bit introspective. Not a gaming media outlet, not a social feed, not a storefront. Closer in spirit to Letterboxd or a well-kept physical journal than to IGN, Steam, or Twitter.

## Anti-references

- **IGN / GameSpot** — flashy game-media energy: banner ads, hype cycles, loud typography, editorial noise
- **Steam store** — transactional, commerce-optimized, utility-first with no personality
- **Generic social media** — infinite scroll, engagement-bait, notification overload, algorithmic feeds that bury personal signal
- **Overwrought gaming aesthetic** — neon on pitch-black, aggressive typography, dragon logos, gaming-chair vibes

## Design Principles

1. **The journal comes first.** Every screen should feel like a thoughtful record, not a feed or a storefront. The emotional frame is "this is mine" — a personal log, not a performance.
2. **Earn trust, don't demand attention.** No dark patterns, no engagement-bait, no red notification bubbles that exist to drive compulsive taps. The app earns loyalty by being genuinely useful and honest.
3. **Quiet authority.** The purple/amber palette should feel calibrated and confident — not loud, not aggressive. Dark and typographic, not neon and aggressive. The app should feel like it knows what it is.
4. **The tool disappears into the task.** Consistent, predictable product patterns. The same button shape. The same form vocabulary. Users should never pause because an affordance looks different from what they expected.
5. **Density when needed, space when not.** Compact and information-rich when a user is in task mode (rating a game, building a list); room to breathe on detail and landing views.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Keyboard navigation throughout. Sufficient contrast on both dark and light themes. Reduced-motion support for all transitions and animations.
